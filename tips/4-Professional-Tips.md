## PART 4: CAREER & PROFESSIONAL PRINCIPLES (25)

### Building Your Reputation

> ***101. Deliver more than you promise*** - Said you'd submit a report by Friday? Submit Wednesday. Said you'd provide a PoC? Provide a test script too. Under-promise and over-deliver every time.

> ***102. Write reports for the judge, not for yourself*** - Judges read hundreds of reports. Make yours skimmable. Use headings. Summarize early. Provide clear severity rationale. They will thank you silently.

> ***103. Acknowledge duplicates gracefully*** - Someone else found your bug first? Congratulate them publicly. Ask to see their approach. Burning bridges over duplicates is expensive. Building bridges is free.

> ***104. Never burn a protocol publicly*** - Found a bug they won't fix? Disclose privately, then walk away. Public shaming might feel good in the moment, but it destroys trust. The security world is small. Reputation follows you.

> ***105. Build a specialized brand*** - "I'm the person who finds oracle manipulation bugs." "I'm the cross-chain replay expert." A brand makes you memorable. Memorable people get invited to private programs.

---

### Navigating Platforms

> ***106. Read every platform's rules twice*** - Scope, eligibility, disclosure policies, payout timelines. One mistake (like public disclosure before permission) can disqualify you. Rules are boring until they cost you money.

> ***107. Build relationships with platform staff*** - Be respectful, responsive, and professional. Platform staff move between companies. They remember good actors. A single positive interaction can lead to years of opportunities.

> ***108. Have a submission template*** - Reusable sections: summary, severity rationale, vulnerable code location, proof of concept, impact, recommendation. Fill in the blanks quickly. Templates save hours.

> ***109. Track your submissions in a spreadsheet*** - Protocol name, date, severity, payout amount (if any), days to response, days to payout. Data reveals patterns. Which platforms pay fastest? Which judges are most responsive?

> ***110. Know when to appeal*** - Some rejections are wrong. If you're certain, politely appeal once. Provide additional evidence. But if they still reject, move on. Two appeals maximum. Your time is valuable.

---

### Financial Discipline

> ***111. Treat bug bounties as bonuses, not salary*** - Variance is extreme. Some months you'll earn $50k. Some months $0. Have a baseline income (freelance, part-time job, savings). Bounties become upside, not rent money.

> ***112. Save aggressively in good months*** - A $100k year might be followed by a $20k year. Save 50%+ of every payout. Build a cushion that lets you hunt without financial pressure. Desperation destroys judgment.

> ***113. Don't chase payouts exclusively*** - Some low-probability bugs have $1 million bounties. Some high-probability bugs have $10k bounties. Expected value matters, not headline numbers. Do the math.

> ***114. Reinvest in your toolkit*** - Buy better hardware. Subscribe to security research platforms. Pay for courses. Attend conferences. Every dollar spent on your education returns multiples.

> ***115. Understand tax implications*** - Bug bounties are taxable income in most jurisdictions. Set aside 30-40% of every payout. Pay estimated quarterly taxes. Tax debt is a killer. Be boring about compliance.

---

### Long-Term Career

> ***116. Build a public presence*** - Write a blog. Tweet findings (after disclosure). Speak at small conferences. A public presence compounds over years. Opportunities find you instead of you finding them.

> ***117. Consider adjacent roles*** - Full-time auditor. Security researcher at a protocol. Consultant. Educator. Bug hunting can lead to other careers. Keep doors open.

> ***118. Learn to estimate and scope*** - Can you review 10,000 lines in two weeks? Can you guarantee coverage of certain invariants? Estimation skills turn hunting into a professional service business.

> ***119. Develop soft skills*** - Communication, negotiation, project management, conflict resolution. Technical skill gets you in the room. Soft skills keep you there.

> ***120. Mentor someone behind you*** - Teaching solidifies your knowledge. It also builds loyalty and community. The person you mentor today might refer you to a million-dollar private program tomorrow.

---

### Ethics & Integrity

> ***121. Never cross into theft*** - Found a live vulnerability? Report it. Don't drain funds to "prove" it. That's not research; that's crime. The line is clear. Stay on the right side.

> ***122. Respect disclosure timelines*** - If a protocol asks for 90 days, give them 90 days. Early disclosure hurts users. Your ego is not worth someone else's money.

> ***123. Don't hoard zero-days*** - Sitting on a critical finding for months while you wait for a bigger payout is unethical. Disclose responsibly. The community depends on good actors.

> ***124. Be honest about severity*** - Don't inflate low findings to critical. Don't bury critical findings in noise. Severity honesty builds trust. Trust unlocks private invites.

> ***125. Leave every protocol better than you found it*** - Even if you find nothing, leave a note: "I checked X, Y, and Z. Everything looked solid." That's professionalism. That's how you get invited back.
